The Stark Reality

A Global Tragedy of Mismatched Logistics

Hunger in our cities is not a problem of food scarcity. It is a failure of communication, rapid transit, and food-safe coordination.

Common Questions

Answers for Chefs, Donors & Non-Profits

Everything you need to know about food safety, legal indemnity, and rapid dispatch.

Yes, 100%. Under the federal Bill Emerson Good Samaritan Food Donation Act (and corresponding international statutes), donors who donate apparently wholesome food in good faith to non-profit organizations are completely protected from liability. In addition, FoodBridge provides a formalized indemnity agreement for every corporate kitchen partner.

We accept:

  • Freshly prepared hot meals: Buffet surplus, banquet batches, rice, curries, roasts maintained at safe holding temperatures.
  • Uncooked produce: Fruits, raw vegetables, and wholesome greens from grocers and wholesale distributors.
  • Artisan baked goods: Breads, bagels, croissants, and sealed pastries from bakeries.
  • Packaged pantry items: Unopened canned goods, juices, and shelf-stable staples before best-by date.

We never accept plate scrapings or food that has sat at room temperature for more than 2 hours.

No. Food donation on FoodBridge is 100% free for all donors. In fact, donating saves commercial kitchens thousands of dollars each month in trash tipping fees, while providing automated charitable tax deduction receipts (80G / 501(c)(3)).

Our average door-to-pickup response time across active city hubs is 38 minutes. For scheduled events (such as weddings, gala dinners, or conferences), pickups can also be booked days in advance for guaranteed dispatch.

Every NGO undergoes a thorough 24-hour verification process including review of their registered non-profit license, food safety certifications, clean refrigeration and warming storage capacity, and beneficiary count verification.
